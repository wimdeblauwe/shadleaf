Sheet

Built from parts

Section titled “Built from parts”

A sheet is a dialog on an edge of the screen, for content that complements the page: filters, settings, the details of a row. It is <sl:sheet> plus the parts you need, as in shadcn/ui: <sl:sheet-header> (holding <sl:sheet-title> and <sl:sheet-description>), your own content and <sl:sheet-footer>.

Everything the dialog page says holds for a sheet: it is the browser’s <dialog> element, buttons open and close it with commandfor and command, the page behind it is inert and does not scroll, Escape and a click outside close it, focus returns to the button that opened it, and open shows one the server renders (an htmx swap). The title and description name and describe it, with ids derived from its id.

The content between the header and the footer gets the sheet’s inline padding, so it lines up with them. The footer stays at the bottom of a sheet on the side of the screen, however little is above it. Content taller than the screen scrolls inside the sheet; put it in <sl:sheet-body> to scroll only the content, with the header and the footer in view.

Side

Section titled “Side”

side is the edge it slides in from: top, right (the default), bottom or left. A sheet on the top or bottom is as wide as the screen and as tall as its content; one on the right or left is as tall as the screen and three quarters of its width, up to a maximum.

right and left are the end and the start of a line, as everywhere in Shadleaf: on a right-to-left page (dir="rtl") a right sheet opens on the left and slides in from there.

The sheet fades in and slides a short distance from its edge. With reduced motion (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) it only fades.

Close buttons

Section titled “Close buttons”

The close button in the corner is on by default; show-close-button="false" leaves it out. <sl:sheet-footer show-close-button> adds an outline “Close” button after the footer’s own actions.

Loaded into the sheet: a body

Section titled “Loaded into the sheet: a body”

<sl:sheet-body> is what htmx loads, as the dialog’s body. You put the sheet in the page once, closed. A request that targets its body (#<sheet id>-body) opens it at once and shows a spinner until the answer is swapped in:

  1. It opens as soon as the request is sent, with the focus in it.
  2. A spinner covers the body while the request runs. It shows only after a moment (200 ms), so a quick answer does not flicker, and the body takes no clicks meanwhile.
  3. The answer is swapped in, and the focus moves to its first field, if it has one.

Closing the sheet while the request still runs aborts it. A body that was empty in the page is emptied again when the sheet closes, so it never shows the previous row while the next one loads.

Show the details of a row

Section titled “Show the details of a row”

  1. Add the sheet to the page, with an empty <sl:sheet-body>. The body’s content gets the id <id>-body, here member-details-body.

  2. Write a fragment with what goes in the body.

  3. Write a controller method that returns the fragment.

  4. Add a button that asks for it with hx:get, with hx-target set to the sheet’s body.

Section titled “A form in the sheet”

htmx fires sl-dialog-close on the form, inside the sheet, which closes; the focus returns to the Edit button. Give that button an id if the row is replaced, so htmx puts the focus back on the new one (see Dialog). A failed save (an error status) shows the error in the sheet and keeps what was typed.

Width

Section titled “Width”

A sheet on the right or left is three quarters of the screen wide, up to a maximum the skin sets (24rem in vega). For a wider one, give it a class and set the maximum in your own stylesheet, loaded after the library’s: the library’s rules sit in a cascade layer, so yours win.

If you compile Tailwind yourself (the embedded bundle), a utility class does the same: class="sm:max-w-2xl".

JavaScript

Section titled “JavaScript”

A sheet uses the dialog’s Alpine component, slDialog; see Dialog.

  • Most of it works without Alpine. Opening from a button, Escape, a click outside and the close buttons are the browser’s. Alpine is needed for open, the sl-dialog-close event, which closes a sheet as it closes a dialog, and a body that htmx loads.
  • It needs no Alpine plugin.
  • Keep your own x-data off <sl:sheet>: it would replace slDialog. Put it on an element inside.
