Toast

One toaster, toasts from the server

Section titled “One toaster, toasts from the server”

A toast says what just happened, such as “Member deleted”, and goes away by itself. shadcn/ui uses Sonner for it, with a <Toaster /> in the layout and toast("...") calls in the browser. In Shadleaf the layout has one <sl:toaster>, and the toasts come from the server, in one of three ways:

Where the message comes from How it gets to the toaster
An htmx request The response header HX-Trigger sends the sl-toast event with the toast as its detail.
A full page load, e.g. after a redirect The page renders <sl:toast>s in the toaster, from a flash attribute.
An htmx response that renders markup anyway An out-of-band swap puts <sl:toast>s into the toaster.

Put the toaster at the end of the layout’s <body>, and let it render the toasts of a flash attribute:

Toast (io.github.wimdeblauwe.shadleaf.toast.Toast) is what a controller writes for either path: a title, an optional description, a variant and an optional duration.

From an htmx response: HX-Trigger

Section titled “From an htmx response: HX-Trigger”

This is what the toast is for: a controller answering an htmx request says what happened, whatever the response swaps. With htmx-spring-boot:

The response gets HX-Trigger: {"sl-toast": {"title": "Ada Lovelace was deleted", "description": null, "variant": "success", "duration": null}}; htmx dispatches the event when the response arrives, and the toaster shows it. Several toasts in one response are a list: htmxResponse.addTrigger("sl-toast", List.of(first, second)).

  • The text is only ever text. The toaster puts the title and description in with textContent, so a name such as <b>Ada</b> shows as typed and can never inject markup.
  • Use HX-Trigger, not HX-Trigger-After-Swap or -After-Settle: those are dispatched on the element that sent the request, and when the swap removed it (a deleted row), the event reaches no one.
  • Not with a redirect. An HX-Redirect or HX-Location loads another page, and the toast with it is gone. Put it in a flash attribute instead (next section).

A screen reader announces such a toast a moment after the page loads (see Announcement), but that moment is also when it starts reading the new page, and not every screen reader lets one interrupt the other. When the outcome is the whole point of the page, say it in the page too: in the title, or in an alert.

The detail is a toast ({title, description, variant, duration}), a list of them, or a string, the title.

Variants

Section titled “Variants”

The variant only changes the icon, as in shadcn: none, a check (success), an i (info), a triangle (warning) and an x (error), whose icon takes the destructive colour. A screen reader announces an error at once, interrupting what it was reading; the others when it is done. Give a toast a title that says what happened on its own (“Could not save”), since the icon is decoration and is not read out.

Timing, pausing and closing

Section titled “Timing, pausing and closing”

A toast closes after 5 seconds (duration on the toaster, or on one toast; 0 keeps it until it is closed). The time stops while the pointer is on the toasts, while one of them has the focus and while the page is in another tab, and at least a second is left afterwards, so nobody loses a toast while reading it. Choose a longer duration, or 0, for a toast with a long description.

  • The close button closes one toast. It needs Alpine, so it only shows once Alpine runs; its label comes from the message sl.toast.close.
  • Alt+T moves the focus to the newest toast, as in Sonner, from anywhere on the page; the toaster’s name, “Notifications (Alt+T)” (message sl.toaster.label), tells screen reader users so. Tab then reaches the close buttons.
  • Escape closes the toast that has the focus, and nothing else: an open dialog around it stays open.
  • When the toast with the focus closes, the focus moves to the next toast, and after the last one back to where it was before.

Several at once

Section titled “Several at once”

Newer toasts stack in front of older ones, as Sonner’s do; hovering or focusing them spreads them out. At most three show (visible-toasts); the older ones wait, with their time stopped, and show again when a newer one closes.

Where the user prefers reduced motion, toasts only fade in and out; they do not slide.

Position

Section titled “Position”

position puts the toasts in a corner or at the middle of the top or bottom edge: top-left, top-center, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-center or bottom-right (the default, as Sonner’s). Right and left are the end and the start of a line, so they swap on a right-to-left page. On a narrow screen the toasts take the full width.

Above a modal dialog

Section titled “Above a modal dialog”

A modal dialog makes the rest of the page inert: it can be seen but not clicked, focused or read by a screen reader, and the dialog is drawn above everything else. The toasts are a popover (in the top layer, above everything), and while a modal dialog is open they move into it, so a toast about something done in a dialog that stays open shows above it, can be closed and is announced. When the dialog closes they move back. A response that closes the dialog and shows a toast (HX-Trigger: {"sl-dialog-close": null, "sl-toast": {...}}) works whichever event comes first.

Announcement

Section titled “Announcement”

The toasts themselves are not a live region, so a screen reader does not read them twice. The toaster has two empty live regions from the start, one polite (role="status") and one assertive (role="alert", for errors), and writes each toast’s title and description into one of them when it arrives. A live region has to be on the page before its content changes for a screen reader to notice, which is why they are in the toaster’s markup rather than added with the toast; a toast rendered with the page is written into them a moment after it loads. The messages are removed from the live regions after a few seconds, so reading the page later finds each toast once, in the “Notifications” region, while it shows. The region is only a landmark while it holds toasts.

Focus after the thing is gone

Section titled “Focus after the thing is gone”

A toast must not take the focus: it goes away, and the focus would fall to the top of the page with it. When an action removes the element that had the focus, such as a Delete button in a table row, move the focus to what comes next and let the toast say what happened. The alert dialog’s htmx example moves it to the next row.

No action button

Section titled “No action button”

Sonner’s toast can hold an action button (“Undo”). Shadleaf’s has none: a toast closes by itself, and whatever it offers is gone with it, for someone who reads slowly, uses a screen reader or looked away. WCAG asks that a time limit on something the user has to do can be turned off or extended. Put the action in the page (an Undo in the row’s place, a link in the confirmation), and use the toast to say what happened. If a toast you render out of band holds a link, the same must be reachable elsewhere, or give it duration="0".

Left out

Section titled “Left out”
  • toast() in the browser. The server sends toasts; a script can dispatch sl-toast (see above).
  • Action and cancel buttons, and promise and loading toasts: see above; a loading state belongs on the button that started the request.
  • Swipe to dismiss. The close button, Escape and the time do it.
  • Rich colours (richColors), expand (always spread out), invert, offset and gap as props: the skins set the look, and the CSS custom properties --toaster-offset and --toast-gap on .toaster-viewport the spacing.
  • Updating a toast by id.

Without JavaScript

Section titled “Without JavaScript”

Without Alpine a toast rendered with the page still shows, in its corner, but it has no close button and does not go away; events and out-of-band toasts need htmx or a script anyway.

sl:toaster

Section titled “sl:toaster”
AttributeValuesDefaultDescription
idstringtoasterThe toaster's id, which an out-of-band swap targets (hx-swap-oob="beforeend:#toaster").
positiontop-left top-center top-right bottom-left bottom-center bottom-rightbottom-rightThe corner or edge the toasts show in. right and left are the end and the start of a line.
durationnumber5000How long a toast shows, in milliseconds, unless it sets its own. The time stops while the pointer is on the toasts, while one of them has the focus and while the page is hidden. 0 keeps toasts until they are closed.
visible-toastsnumber3How many toasts show at once, the newest ones. Older ones wait, with their time stopped, and show again when a newer one closes.

Every prop can also be set from an expression: th:id="${...}". Any other attribute passes through to the rendered element, and class is appended.

Section titled “sl:toast”
AttributeValuesDefaultDescription

JavaScript

Section titled “JavaScript”

A small Alpine component, slToaster, is on <sl:toaster>: it renders as <div class="toaster" x-data="slToaster">.

  • It needs no Alpine plugin. If you load Alpine yourself (shadleaf.assets.alpine=external), plain Alpine is enough. See Alpine.
  • Keep your own x-data off <sl:toaster>. An element has one x-data.
  • One toaster per page. A second one works, but the first takes every event.
  • htmx history: the toaster removes its toasts before htmx saves the page in its history cache, so going back never shows an old toast again.
Event On Effect
sl-toast anything (it bubbles to the document), or the document Shows the toast in its detail: {title, description, variant, duration}, a list of them, or a string.
